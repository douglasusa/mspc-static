Main Street Presbyterian Church · Columbus, Mississippi

Revitalization 2029

A $1.8 million renewal of the buildings that carry the ministry of a congregation approaching its two hundredth year.

Celebrating 200 years of faith, fellowship, and service.

Why Now

Nearly two centuries in one place

Main Street Presbyterian Church has stood in Columbus, Mississippi since 1829 — nearly two centuries of worship, teaching, and service on one campus, without interruption. That continuity is the plainest fact about this congregation and the hardest thing about it to replace.

In 2029 the church will mark its two hundredth anniversary. The elders, deacons, and Revitalization 2029 committee recognize in that milestone both a meaningful opportunity and a sacred responsibility. The buildings that have carried this ministry now require significant renewal, and the congregation has chosen to address that need before the anniversary rather than after it.

The sanctuary has not had a major update since 1995. The scope the committee adopted reaches roofing, windows, flooring, lighting, restrooms, signage, heating and cooling, and audio and video systems across the campus — the ordinary fabric of buildings in continuous use. The repair and new-construction areas were photographed on site, and the work was priced by the project architect rather than estimated in the abstract.

Nothing in the scope was chosen for appearance alone. Each item is there because a ministry the congregation already performs depends on it.

The Purpose

To renew the physical spaces that support the ministries of Main Street Presbyterian Church

Revitalization 2029 is not about luxury. The campaign names seven purposes and answers to them: worship, welcome, fellowship, care, service, education, and formation.

Through improvements to the welcome area, sanctuary, office area, Bateman Building, and Villee Building, the campaign will help create spaces that are safer, more accessible, more functional, and inviting. These renewed spaces will serve not only the needs of today's congregation, but also the ministry opportunities God is preparing for the future.

“We will not neglect the house of our God.”

Nehemiah 10:39
The Work

Five areas, one renewal

Each renewed space has its own purpose, yet together they help the church worship with reverence, welcome with warmth, teach with effectiveness, gather in fellowship, care for neighbors, and reach those in need. The scope below is drawn from the campaign documents and the architect's plans.

01

A New Welcome and Greeting AreaThe threshold

A revitalized welcome and greeting area is the threshold through which many people first encounter the spirit of Main Street Presbyterian. The new space turns first impressions into ministry: before worship begins, Christ-centered hospitality helps members, families, neighbors, guests, and seekers feel welcomed, guided, and at home.

The renovation takes in the current church office, the small sitting room, and the floor and rooms above them. In the architect's plan, an entry, secretary's office, sitting room, and parlour become a welcome centre with a welcome desk, a coffee service, and a seating area. Its value is not cosmetic — it is pastoral and missional. A clearer, more accessible greeting space reduces confusion and removes barriers, strengthening the sense of belonging.

02

The SanctuaryThe spiritual heart

The sanctuary is the spiritual heart of the church, where generations have met God in prayer, Word, sacrament, song, grief, joy, and hope. Renewing it preserves the place where God's people are shaped, comforted, and strengthened — and prepares future generations to hear and embrace the Gospel.

After nearly three decades since its last major update in 1995, the sanctuary is ready for a thoughtful renewal: fresh paint, new carpet, updated pew cushions, enhanced lighting, improved air conditioning and heating, and strengthened sound and video capabilities. These updates will bring greater clarity, comfort, and beauty to worship for those in the pews and those joining online.

03

Ministry and Office SpacesThe work behind the work

The renovated choir room and renewed offices sustain the quiet, faithful work behind visible ministry: leadership, pastoral stewardship, care, communication, planning, and service. They remind us that administration becomes ministry when it turns prayer into care, love into action, and vision into faithful practice.

The plan repurposes the choir room and creates a new church office and a new pastor's office positioned near the new parking entrance. Where the north administrative wing now holds a choir room, classrooms, and offices, the renovation provides a prayer chapel, reception, conference room, pastor's office, work and storage space, and controlled access. These improvements strengthen the church's capacity for care and effectiveness, supporting the leadership and coordination of its services and ministries.

04

The Bateman BuildingFellowship and service

The Bateman Building embodies the congregation's commitment to feed, host, teach, serve, organise, and encourage. A revitalized Bateman Building will deepen fellowship, hospitality, community support, and practical ministries through the love of Christ.

Renewing this place honors the gatherings, meals, meetings, ministries, and acts of care that have formed this congregation — and strengthens the space where future families will gather, serve, and grow. The work includes an updated kitchen and fellowship hall, and the building's share of the modernization program described below.

05

The Villee BuildingEducation and formation

Renewing the Villee Building strengthens the ministry of education, accessibility, and future growth — passing faith to the next generation. It prepares a place where children, youth, adults, and families learn Scripture, ask questions, deepen relationships, and grow as disciples of Christ.

It also ensures that Main Street Christian School, which the building houses, will continue responding faithfully to the needs of the community and fulfil the outreach the congregation desires. Renewing the Villee Building is an investment in those who will carry this church's witness into the future.

Modernisation across both buildings

The Bateman and Villee Buildings are ready for modernization and interior updates. New flooring, fresh paint, enhanced lighting and new ceiling tiles, improved air conditioning and heating, an updated kitchen and fellowship hall, new windows, and a replaced roof will strengthen the Bateman Building for fellowship and service, and renew the Villee Building as an investment in those who will carry its witness into the future.

Faithful Stewardship

An inheritance received, and an inheritance to leave

Nothing about this campus was inevitable. Every generation that worshiped here paid to keep it standing, and each one handed the next a building it had not built. That privilege now belongs to this generation, and with it the same responsibility.

This is why the committee describes Revitalization 2029 as an act of stewardship rather than a construction project. Gratitude for nearly two centuries of God’s care and commitment to the ministry still ahead are one disposition facing in two directions.

A congregation cannot renew a building for itself alone. Most of the use this work will see belongs to people not yet in these pews: children now learning the faith, families not yet arrived, and the bicentennial congregation that will inherit whatever this one leaves it.

“So often, under the providence of God, we stand on the shoulders of those who went before us.”

Dr. Nicholas Needham
