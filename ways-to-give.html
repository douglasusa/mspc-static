Revitalization 2029 · The Pledge

Ways to give

As Main Street Presbyterian Church prepares to celebrate 200 years of God’s sustaining grace in 2029, please prayerfully consider contributing to the Revitalization 2029 campaign.

Your gift will help renew the spaces that support worship, welcome, fellowship, teaching, care, service, and future growth.

Record a pledge

Four Routes

How gifts reach the church

All contributions are made directly to Main Street Presbyterian Church. Choose whichever route suits your circumstances; the church treats them identically.

01

By check

Make your check payable to Main Street Presbyterian Church and note Revitalization 2029. Mail it with your pledge card to:

Main Street Presbyterian Church
ATTN: “Revitalization 2029”
P.O. Box 26
Columbus, MS 39703

02

Online through the church

The church receives online gifts through its own giving portal. Payment is handled by the church, not on this site.

Give through Realm

03

Stock, donor-advised fund, or other non-cash gift

These gifts are arranged directly with the church. Please contact Henry Pilkinton or Danny Williams to make the transfer.

Gifts of appreciated assets may carry particular tax advantages. See the tax guidance.

04

In person

You may hand your completed pledge card to Henry Pilkinton or Danny Williams at the church.

What happens to the information you submit

A pledge recorded here is delivered to the Revitalization 2029 campaign records so the committee can plan against known commitments. DOUGLAS USA LLC Fundraising maintains this site on the church’s behalf and does not receive, hold, or administer contributions. Gifts are made directly to Main Street Presbyterian Church, a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization, EIN 64-0350400, and are deductible to the extent permitted by law. The Invitation

A pledge is a small act of planning that makes a larger act of faith possible.

Recording an intention costs nothing and obliges nothing. It simply tells the committee what it may count on as the work is scheduled — and it gives a household time to give well rather than quickly.
